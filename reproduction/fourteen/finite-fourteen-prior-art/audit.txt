Erdős–Hammer finite polygon minima: fourteen-point prior-art audit, 2026-11-20

The target counts convex-position subsets of at least three vertices, irrespective of points interior to the selected polygon. A fourteen-point set contributes 14 singletons and 91 pairs. Thus polygon totals 778,779,780,781,782 correspond to 883,884,885,886,887 nonempty subsets or 884,885,886,887,888 subsets including the empty set. For n=10,...,13 the polygon prefix 189,283,408,570 converts to 244,349,486,661 or 245,350,487,662 respectively.

No pre-existing exact fourteen-point total, or explicitly identified upper witness at any of the five target totals, was found in the following searches. This is a bounded literature and data search, not a proof of priority or of minimum value.
* The original question asks for the asymptotic total (Erdős, 1978, pp. 52–53); the public problem discussion did not report a fourteen-point total. https://users.renyi.hu/~p_erdos/1978-44.pdf and https://www.erdosproblems.com/forum/thread/838 .
* Savova's public research release is a single 2026-09-24 commit. Exact finite certificates end at 11. Searched both public #838 archive trees (1,403 files apiece, with overlapping contents) and searched numerical proximity to fourteen-point labels in 772 archived .md/.tex/.txt/.json files under 3.2 MB. The paper's finite_size_table.py concerns explicit Pascal-template constructions. https://github.com/NikolSavova/erdos-838-convex-subsets/commit/52159227983814f01cddb6478234eba2b8d3af15 ; https://github.com/NikolSavova/proof-hunter/tree/main/archive/phase2/loop/erdos838 ; https://github.com/NikolSavova/proof_hunter_working/tree/main/phase2/loop/erdos838 .
* DataCite DOI-title search for “convex polygons” with “fourteen” or “thirteen” found only the two version/concept DOIs for the 12–13 result, https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.24388070 and https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.24388069 . Direct Zenodo API search was blocked; DataCite supplied the independent DOI check. Public GitHub repository/commit queries using problem numbers, target numerals and title terms did not turn up an earlier 14-point claim. English and Spanish/French/German phrase variants were also searched.
* The entire OEIS stripped sequence file dated 2026-11-19 gave no contiguous match to any of the three known four-term prefixes above. https://oeis.org/stripped.gz .
* Aichholzer/Krasser extended the realizable order-type database to 11 and used a different extremal computation for rectilinear crossings: https://doi.org/10.1016/j.comgeo.2005.07.005 (publisher page https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0925772106000460). Rectilinear crossings count convex quadrilaterals only. The 2026-08-04 public 14-point coordinates in https://github.com/rhyschappell/rcn-records/blob/c711665081d6cb320af57da2b2bb9d769f5c05e3/records/n014.asc give the independently computed polygon profile 364,324,156,40,4 (sizes 3–7) and total 888. For every 14-subset in each of that archive's configurations of sizes 15–20, the least polygon totals respectively were 872,873,863,856,839,830.
* Subercaseaux et al. establish mu_5(14)=42 for pentagons alone. Enumerating all 120 two-point deletions of the sixteen-point pinwheel defined in their Section 5.1 gave minimum total 923, with profile 364,511,42,6. https://www.cs.cmu.edu/~mheule/publications/CICM24.pdf ; https://github.com/bsubercaseaux/minimize-5gons .
* Huemer et al. establish weighted identities among numbers of convex polygons by their number of interior points; their displayed finite example has ten points. https://doi.org/10.1007/s00454-022-00395-8 . The separate July #838 working report does not certify a fourteen-point total: https://www.erdosproblemaday.com/report/838 .

The recounts on existing coordinates are computations made in this audit. An old point configuration might implicitly realize a result without its total having been tabulated; sparse keyword searches cannot exclude such cases.
