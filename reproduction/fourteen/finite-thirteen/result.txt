The minimum number of convex polygons on thirteen planar points is 570

Every set of thirteen planar points, no three collinear, determines at
least 570 convex polygons. A polygon is specified by a subset of at least
three points, all vertices of its convex hull. Unselected points can lie
inside. Up to relabeling and mirror reflection exactly five order types
achieve equality. Their polygon counts are:

  types   triangles  quadrilaterals  pentagons  hexagons  size >=7  total
    1        286          241            41         2        0       570
    4        286          247            35         2        0       570

Upper bound. witness/witness13.txt is an integer configuration of the
first type. The standard-library witness/verify.py computes each subset's
convex hull by signed turns. For all five types see
certificates/grid-realizations.jsonl. The independent standard-library
certificates/verify-realizations.py checks the full orientation signatures
of these configurations, canonicalizes over hull vertices, and recounts
their polygons by convex hull.

Lower bound and completeness. Write Q and P for the numbers of convex
quadrilaterals and pentagons. There are 286 triangles. Thus every
configuration with at most 570 polygons satisfies Q+P<=284. The weighted
deletion identity ensures that such a configuration can be grown through
a nested sequence with the bounds reported in deletion/candidates-284.txt.
In detail, if A Q+B P<=T for m points, some deletion satisfies
  A(m-5) Q' + B(m-4) P' <= floor(T(m-4)(m-5)/m).
This follows by averaging because each quadrilateral survives m-4
deletions and each pentagon survives m-5; at m=5 simply use the
quadrilateral count. Divide by the gcd and round down as needed.

Starting with a triangle, extend all surviving orientation signatures by
one point in every possible ray sector around each old point. For a fixed
old point i, a new direction crosses one of the old lines ij each time
one sign changes. Its sign pattern is therefore represented just to one
side of an old ray or its antipode. Agreement of [ijx] and -[jix] is
necessary. Reject sign rows which would give a four-point affine circuit
all of one sign; a two-positive, two-negative circuit marks a convex
quadrilateral. A larger set is convex precisely when all its four-subsets
are convex by planar Carathéodory. Canonicalization uses actual relabelings
and simultaneous reversal of all signs; it never merges inequivalent
realizable configurations. This may retain patterns incompatible with the
plane but covers every realizable extension. The C++17 implementation is
deletion/certificate.cpp. Its smaller predecessors and ray-sector proof
are in ../finite-eleven/deletion/proof.txt and ../finite-twelve/deletion/.

At order thirteen 625 signatures pass Q+P<=284. Counting all polygons
leaves one possible signature at 569 and ten at 570. There is none below
569. The 569 signature would have 286 triangles, 245 convex quadrilaterals,
37 convex pentagons, and one larger polygon. It is impossible in the
real plane. Its nine-row certificate in certificates/compact.txt uses the
three-term identity
  [abc][ade] - [abd][ace] + [abe][acd] = 0,
where [abc] is a signed doubled triangle area. When one summand has
opposite sign to the other two, its magnitude strictly exceeds each of
their magnitudes. The nine forced strict comparisons multiply to X>X with
X positive and all area factors cancelling. A separate run at Q+P<=283,
the exact necessary threshold for at most 569 polygons, leaves the very
same single signature after the polygon-count filter.

Of the ten 570 candidates, five have similar impossible area certificates
and five have the verified integer realizations above. All six exclusions
are in certificates/compact-proofs.jsonl with the exact input signs and
positive multiplicities. The five canonical realized signatures are
distinct. This proves the value and classifies all minimizers.

Replay commands from finite-thirteen, using a build directory on the SSD:
  TMPDIR="$PWD/deletion" clang++ -O3 -std=c++17 deletion/certificate.cpp -o deletion/verify
  (set -o pipefail; deletion/verify 13 284 | cmp - deletion/candidates-284.txt)
  (set -o pipefail; deletion/verify 13 283 | cmp - deletion/candidates-283.txt)
  python3 -B witness/verify.py
  python3 -B certificates/check.py --certificates certificates/compact-proofs.jsonl
  python3 -B certificates/verify-realizations.py
The two pipeline commands use bash or zsh. The certificate discovery solvers
are optional; replay after compilation uses only the Python standard library.

Scope. The July 2026 report https://www.erdosproblemaday.com/report/838
ends at nine. Nikol Savova's September 2026 paper extends the known
values to eleven: https://github.com/NikolSavova/erdos-838-convex-subsets .
We found no prior report of the thirteen-point total or the five-type classification;
the searches do not certify historical priority. Erdős's 1978 limiting
question remains open. Exhaustiveness is supported by the deletion proof
and its implementation; the enumeration itself runs outside Lean.

Formal checks. witness/Witness13.lean checks the exact profile and general
position of the displayed witness in Lean's kernel. formal/Exclude569.lean
and formal/OtherExclusions.lean prove that no configuration of thirteen
real planar points realizes any of the six excluded 286-bit signatures.
The 569 theorem uses nine strict determinant-product inequalities; its
hypothesis is the actual signature. formal/replay.sh reruns these proofs,
and formal/replay.log records their axiom reports.
